### Factor f1, positive pole: **Sociohistorical-critical justification of inclusive education**

An alternative concise label is **Theory-grounded, socially situated curriculum discourse**. The pole characterises curriculum writing that *explains and legitimises* educational choices through pedagogical theory, accounts of how learners are historically and socially constituted, and commitments to participation and inclusion.

The corpus-wide loadings support this reading. Strong terms such as *dialético*, *sócio-histórico*, *histórico-crítico*, *totalidade*, *hegemônico* and *problematização* frame education as a matter for critical social analysis. *Teórico-metodológico*, *categoria*, *teoria*, *abordagem* and references to Saviani, Freire and Morin signal sustained conceptual justification. Alongside them, *sujeito*, *ensino-aprendizagem*, *dialógico*, *democratização*, *exclusão*, *dignidade* and *equidade* connect that reasoning to learners and educational rights. The high loading of *que* also points to extended explanatory prose, but is not itself a thematic marker.

The Santa Catarina excerpts show how this discourse works across subjects, rather than defining it as a single topic. One grounds humanities teaching in conceptual *categorias*, *raciocínio* and the *dignidade* of historically situated *sujeitos*; another uses *problematização*, *singularidade* and *equidade* to argue for recognising diverse youth. The Arte, language and literacy passages likewise justify teaching through *percepção*, *inter-relação*, *constitutivo* and *sujeito*. Some high-scoring samples are primarily course-organisation tables, so their administrative content should not be mistaken for the pole’s defining discourse; the loadings and the substantive passages provide the stronger interpretive evidence.

**Santa Catarina appears to drive the illustrated extreme:** all ten supplied high-scoring excerpts are from SC, which also has the highest state mean (24.02). Paraná (23.85) and Pará (23.59) score nearly as highly, so the dimension is not confined to SC. Ceará (20.09), Bahia (19.30), Maranhão (18.28) and Mato Grosso (18.07) are also relatively strong. The pattern crosses regions rather than characterising one uniformly: for example, SC and PR score strongly within the South, while Rio Grande do Sul does not show a comparable mean.
