### Factor f2 (positive): **Regulated curricular provision and implementation**

This pole encodes a discourse that turns educational aims into **authorised, organised provision**: who is responsible, which rules apply, what courses or modalities may be offered, and how their hours, pathways and credentials are specified. The strongest loadings combine administrative and legal authority (*secretaria, secretário, CEE, CNE, portaria, regulamentação, legislação*) with operational terms (*oferta, ofertar, carga, semestre, horário, municípios*) and educational pathways (*EPT, curso, habilitação, qualificação, certificação*). Together, they point to curriculum as something to be governed and put into effect, not to a single subject area or learner group.

The high-scoring excerpts illustrate that broader pattern. Santa Catarina texts cite *portaria, CNE, LDB, diretriz* and *artigo* to authorise New Secondary Education arrangements; specify *carga* and *itinerários*; and explain how technical *cursos* lead to *habilitação, qualificação* and *certificação*. Other excerpts extend the same governing-and-provisioning language to EJA, Indigenous and Quilombola education, using terms such as *oferta, legislação, municípios* and *regulamentação*. Those modalities therefore qualify the interpretation: this is not exclusively a discourse of secondary-school reform or vocational training. Nor should the excerpts’ substantive discussions of identity and rights be mistaken for the factor’s dominant lexical signal.

Santa Catarina has the highest mean (9.91), and **all supplied examples come from that state**, so it is the clearest illustration but cannot stand for every high-scoring stratum. Sergipe (9.87), Bahia (9.82), Maranhão (9.49), Pará (9.24) and Tocantins (9.09) also score strongly; Mato Grosso do Sul (8.88) follows. This suggests a presence across several northern and northeastern states as well as Santa Catarina, **not a uniform regional pattern**.

Possible shorter labels are **“Administrative implementation of educational provision”** or **“Regulation of curricular pathways and provision.”** Both capture the pole’s recurring function: grounding varied educational offerings in institutional authority and specifying how they are to operate.
