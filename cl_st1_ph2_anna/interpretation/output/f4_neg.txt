### Factor f4 (negative pole): **Competency-based curriculum design and learning assessment**

This pole is best read as discourse that **specifies how learning is organised, developed and assessed**. Across the corpus, the strongest loadings combine assessment terms—*somativo, autoavaliação, diagnosticar, verificação, mensurar*—with terms for sequenced learning and curricular specification—*progressividade, gradação, gradual, bloom, taxonomia, explicitação, complemento*. *Tutoria, fgb* and the technical-training abbreviations suggest that this discourse can also address support arrangements and particular curriculum pathways. The loadings therefore support a broader interpretation than “assessment” alone.

The excerpts show how these strands work together. Paraná texts describe competencies as progressively developed skills, including an account of cognitive levels associated with *Bloom* and *taxonomia*. Others set out diagnostic, formative and summative assessment, with *autoavaliação* and *verificação* used to identify learning and guide subsequent teaching. The repeated loading *somativo* does **not** imply advocacy of summative testing: one excerpt explicitly cautions against treating it as the only form of assessment. References to *fgb*, *progressividade* and *gradual* place these practices within the organisation of Ensino Médio learning pathways.

**Paraná appears to drive the illustrated negative pole most clearly**: it has the lowest state mean (0.427), and all ten supplied negative-scoring excerpts are from Paraná. Other relatively low means occur in Acre, the Federal District, Rondônia, Amapá and São Paulo. All state means are positive, however, so these are *relative* positions on the negative pole, not negative state averages. The means do not indicate a simple regional tendency: for example, Santa Catarina scores substantially higher than Paraná despite also being in the South.

Possible concise labels are **“Competency-based learning progression and assessment”** or **“Curricular specification and assessment of learning.”** The first best captures the recurring link between staged competencies and evidence of student development; the second better accommodates the pole’s curriculum-organisation and technical-pathway vocabulary beyond the assessment-focused samples.
