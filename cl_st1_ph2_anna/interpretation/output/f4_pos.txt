### Factor f4 (positive): **Territorialising the curriculum through regional heritage**

This pole encodes a discourse that makes a state’s **landscapes, peoples and cultural practices** into resources for describing the region and contextualising education. The strongest loadings combine physical geography and ecology (*mata, agreste, litoral, fauna, lagoa, sertão, flora, floresta*) with heritage and identity (*artesanato, folclore, coco, indígena, ancestralidade, racial*). Place names and population terms locate those resources in particular territories. Loadings such as *amazônia, venezuelano, migrante* and *migração* also show that the corpus-wide pattern extends beyond the Alagoas settings prominent in the examples.

The high-scoring Alagoas excerpts illustrate two connected functions. Territorial surveys organise the state by coast, forest, agreste and sertão, linking *litoral, mata, fauna, flora, rio* and *turismo* to environments and livelihoods. Curriculum passages then turn such descriptions into local teaching content: *artesanato, folclore, coco, indígena* and *território* appear in discussions of arts, diverse identities and regionally contextualised learning. Some excerpts additionally address environmental degradation and social injustice; the pole is therefore not simply a celebration of scenery or tradition.

**Alagoas** has the highest state mean (2.69) and supplies all the illustrated texts, though its large SD (5.80) signals considerable variation. **Pará** (2.63) and **Santa Catarina** (2.42) are also strong, followed by **Piauí** (1.80); the pattern is not confined to one Brazilian region. The label thus describes the shared discourse-functional tendency, rather than treating the Alagoas examples as its full range.
