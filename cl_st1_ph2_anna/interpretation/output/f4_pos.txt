## Factor f4 (positive): **Place-based cultural recognition in education**

A possible alternative label is **Territorial diversity and inclusive curriculum**. This pole brings local landscapes, populations and histories into curricular discussion, often to argue that education should recognise the identities, knowledge and rights of the people who live there.

The full-corpus loadings give the dimension its breadth. Terms for peoples and social histories—*índio, quilombo, imigrante, pardo, indígena, migrante, ancestralidade*—occur alongside place and environment—*litoral, mar, praia, agreste, sertão, floresta, rio, semiárido, amazônico, território, territorialidade*. *Artesanato, folclore, coco* and *culinário* point to cultural practices, while *racial, racismo, xenofobia, discriminação* and *exclusão* indicate that recognition also entails confronting inequality. The coastal, Northeastern and migration-related loadings are important: the pole is broader than an Amazonian discourse.

The excerpts show how this vocabulary functions in high-scoring texts. Pará’s curriculum connects *floresta, ribeirinho, indígena, rio* and *territorialidade* to the lived knowledge of communities and to educational provision. Passages on quilombola and ethnic-racial education link *quilombo, ancestralidade, território, identidade* and *racismo* to curricular recognition and rights. Geography and rural-education passages similarly treat territory as a lived, contested space, not simply a location. These examples strongly illustrate a critical, sometimes decolonial articulation of the pole; they should not make that stance a requirement of every text represented by the wider loadings.

**Pará appears to be the clearest driver**: it has the highest state mean (5.03), and every supplied high-scoring excerpt comes from Pará. Bahia (4.58) and Santa Catarina (3.72) also score notably highly, followed by Alagoas (3.17), Maranhão (3.04) and Goiás (2.95). The elevated scores across different regions suggest a discourse of *regional particularity and social diversity*, rather than one confined to the North. Because the examples are exclusively Paraense, they establish the pole’s Amazonian expression especially well, while the loadings and state means warrant the broader label.
