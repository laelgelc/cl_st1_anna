### Factor f5 (negative): **Sociocritical, territorially situated curriculum discourse**

This pole frames curriculum as a socially and politically situated project. Across the corpus, loadings such as *ético-político*, *societário*, *omnilateral*, *histórico-crítico*, *globalidade* and *constructo* point to theoretical reflection on education and social formation. *Erradicação*, *intolerância*, *terreiro*, *periferia*, *raça* and *dignidade* add a concern with inequality, cultural recognition and rights. *Transversalidade* and *intersetorial* suggest that these concerns are to run through curricular practice rather than remain separate topics.

The excerpts show how that discourse works in high-scoring texts. Bahia’s guidelines theorise curriculum and its local enactment; present diversity, ethnic-racial relations, adult education and environmental education as matters of rights and social transformation; and treat territory and childhood as contexts for curricular decisions. Loading words visible in these passages—including *omnilateral*, *intolerância*, *periferia*, *transversalidade*, *societário* and *engendrar*—connect the sampled discussions to the wider loading pattern. Repeated *bahia* and citations of *Macedo* also indicate a distinctive documentary and theoretical vocabulary, not just a general theme.

**Bahia is the clearest state driver**: it has the lowest mean (0.10), and all supplied high-scoring excerpts come from Bahia. Rio Grande do Sul (0.53), Ceará (0.62) and Pará (0.72) are next lowest, but the means do not establish a consistent regional grouping; all state means are positive, so the distinction is relative.

Possible shorter labels are **“Sociocritical curriculum grounding”** or **“Curriculum as situated social transformation.”** The first best captures both the pole’s theoretical vocabulary and the excerpts’ use of that vocabulary to justify inclusive, locally responsive educational practice.
